Background jazz vs dance music

It is the most important decision of the evening and it comes before choosing a band: should the music create atmosphere, or get people out of their chairs?

DK
David Kleňha
Jazz singer & bandleader · 7 min read · 8 August 2026

When an organiser books "a jazz band", they may mean two entirely different things. One plays so guests barely notice it. The other plays so nobody is still sitting after half an hour. We do both, but they are different evenings — and different line-ups.

Background jazz: what makes it good

Background music is not music at half effort. It is a discipline of its own, and the point is to hold a steady level — no dynamic jumps, no solos that grab attention, no songs people start humming.

  • Bossa nova — the most reliable genre for a reception. Even rhythm, no surprises.
  • Ballads and mid tempos — Fly Me to the Moon, Summertime, The Girl from Ipanema.
  • Instrumental blocks — part of the programme without vocals, so the music does not compete with conversation.
  • No drums — a duo or trio. The double bass adds bottom end without adding volume.

Dance music: what makes it work

A dance floor is not filled by volume but by rhythm and set construction. Three things matter:

  • Drums. Without them people do not stand up. The minimum is a quartet.
  • Familiar songs. People come to the floor for what they know — swing evergreens, L-O-V-E, Sway, Cheek to Cheek, latin and funk.
  • Set construction. The first dance block must not open with the fastest number. Build up gradually and save the strongest material for the second half.

For large corporate parties and gala evenings, a quintet adds a horn section — saxophone, clarinet or trumpet on request. That is a sound the room recognises immediately.

From experience

When the floor stays empty even with a quartet

When the dance block is scheduled for 21:00 but dinner overruns to 22:15. Guests have been sitting for two hours and the energy is gone. The answer is not a louder band but moving one set before dinner.

Combining both in one evening

Most corporate events need both — which is exactly why a band is booked instead of a playlist. The scenario that works:

That transition is a matter of experience, not repertoire. The band has to sense when the room is ready — and when it is not.

How to decide

  • Is there a dance floor? If not, there will be no dancing, whoever plays.
  • When does the programme end? Events finishing by 22:00 usually do not get to dancing.
  • Who is the audience? A networking reception and a Christmas party are two different worlds, even from the same company.

If in doubt, book a trio and agree that the last set will be more danceable. It costs less than a quartet playing background music all evening.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between background jazz and dance music?

Background jazz is played as a duo or trio without drums, in calmer tempos and at a volume that allows conversation at the table. Dance music needs drums, faster tempos and more familiar songs, so the minimum line-up is a quartet.

Will the dance floor fill without drums?

Usually not. Drums hold the tempo and give the music the drive people need to get up. If you expect dancing, we recommend at least a quartet.

Can background music and dancing be combined in one evening?

Yes, and that is the most common corporate scenario. It starts with calm jazz for arrival and dinner, then the programme gradually lifts towards a dance block after the formal part.

Which genre suits a reception best?

Bossa nova. It has an even rhythm with no dynamic jumps, so it does not pull attention and guests converse easily over it.

What does a background band cost compared with a dance band?

A duo or trio for background costs from CZK 15,000 for a standard evening; a quartet or quintet for dancing costs from CZK 27,000. Prices are for Prague and include our own PA.
