Balls & galas

A band for your ball or gala dinner in Prague

A ball band has a different job from a corporate party band: guests have come to dance, and the music has to fit around the programme. Here is which line-up suits your hall and how to plan the evening.

DK
David Kleňha
Jazz singer & bandleader · 8 min read · 7 November 2026

In the Czech Republic, ball season runs from January to March. Whether it is a city ball or a company gala, the evening has a fixed running order — opening, dance display, raffle, awards — and the music has to pick up after each item without dead air. That is why we play balls with larger line-ups: with drums, or with a full horn section.

The Jazz Combo is our largest line-up: vocals, piano, double bass, drums and four horns — trumpet, trombone and two saxophones. It plays big band arrangements with a line-up that fits an ordinary hall, and the whole band performs in matching outfits.

The quartet with drums has a full, rhythmic sound that gets the dance floor moving. We recommend it as the main band for smaller halls; without drums, a ball crowd dances far less.

The trio works as a second stage. It plays during dinner, in the lounge or by the bar while the main hall dances to a band with drums.

How to plan the music around the programme

A ball has a fixed schedule, and the music adapts to it, not the other way round. Send us the running order as early as you can: we plan the dance sets around it and agree who gives the band its cues.

  • Guests arriving. Relaxed swing and ballads, so people can greet each other and talk.
  • Opening. The first number after the host's welcome. If you want a specific dance to open the ball, put it in your enquiry.
  • Dance sets. Swing, latin and slow ballads alternate so the floor never empties.
  • Programme items. Dance display, raffle or awards. The band finishes the song and picks up as soon as the item ends.
  • Finale. The last set is built on the best-known swing numbers.

A standard evening is 2 hours of live music with one 15-minute break; at most we play 3 hours with two breaks. We do not play longer than 3 hours. A ball usually runs longer, so we place our sets at the main dance moments of your schedule. Recorded music or a DJ booked by the organiser can cover the rest of the night.

Music for a ball: swing, latin and ballads

Our dance sets are built on swing and jazz standards arranged for horns, with latin, ballads and Czech classics in between. From the Jazz Combo repertoire, for example:

  • Swing: In the Mood, It Don't Mean a Thing, Mack the Knife, Sway, Theme from New York, New York
  • Latin: The Girl from Ipanema, Quando, Quando, Quando, Perhaps, Perhaps, Perhaps
  • Slow dances: At Last, Moon River, When I Fall in Love

The difference between a listening set and a dance set is explained in background jazz vs dance music.

From experience

Two stages at one ball

At a large ball it pays to have the main band in the hall and a smaller line-up in the lounge or by the bar. Guests who want to talk have somewhere to go, and the main dance floor stays full.

What the band needs at the venue

  • Floor area. About 6 × 4 m for the Jazz Combo, 4 × 3 m for the quartet. A stage is ideal; a level, dry surface is a must.
  • Sound. The quartet brings its own BOSE PA for events of up to 80 to 100 guests; a larger hall needs a sound engineer. The Jazz Combo with horns always plays with a sound engineer: we provide one, or work with the venue's technician.
  • Power. A power supply right by the stage.
  • Set-up time. We arrive early to set up and sound-check, so everything is ready before the first guests come in.
  • Dress. The Jazz Combo wears matching outfits, black formal wear as standard. On request we match the dress code or the colours of your ball.

Full technical requirements are in what the band needs on site, and how many musicians per guests covers line-up size by hall.

Line-up and price

The Jazz Combo starts from CZK 50,000 for a standard evening with the full eight-piece line-up. Smaller line-ups are quoted individually and the PA with a sound engineer according to the venue. The quartet costs from CZK 27,000 including our own BOSE PA, the trio from CZK 20,000.

A standard evening means 2 hours of live music with one 15-minute break and transport within Prague. Outside Prague, transport is charged per vehicle: CZK 7/km for the trio, CZK 14/km for the quartet and CZK 21/km for the Jazz Combo. A third hour of music is priced depending on the date; we do not play longer than 3 hours. All prices are in our price list.

Frequently asked questions

How much does a ball band cost in Prague?

The Jazz Combo starts from CZK 50,000 and the quartet from CZK 27,000 for a standard evening of 2 hours of live music with one break. For the Combo, the PA with a sound engineer is quoted according to the venue. We play for a maximum of 3 hours with two breaks. We send a quote within 24 hours.

How long do you play at a ball?

As standard 2 hours with one break, at most 3 hours with two breaks. We do not play longer than 3 hours, so we place our sets at the main dance moments of the ball. Recorded music or a DJ booked by the organiser can cover the rest of the night.

Which band suits a city or formal ball?

The Jazz Combo. Four horns fill a large hall, and big band arrangements work for listening and dancing alike. For a smaller company or hotel ball, the quartet with drums is enough.

When should we book a band for the ball season?

As early as possible. The Czech ball season runs from January to March, and its Fridays and Saturdays go first, so it is worth enquiring several months ahead.

Can you provide sound for the whole hall?

For the Jazz Combo, yes: we provide a PA with a sound engineer for an extra charge depending on the hall size and guest count, or work with the venue's engineer. The quartet brings its own BOSE PA for events of up to 80 to 100 guests.

Do you play balls outside the Czech Republic?

We are based in Prague and play across the Czech Republic; events in Germany and Austria are possible by arrangement.
