Capacity

How many musicians for how many guests

Band size is not driven by budget but by guest count, room size and acoustics. Here are the numbers you can actually decide on.

DK
David Kleňha
Jazz singer & bandleader · 8 min read · 8 August 2026

The question I get most often from event managers is: "Will a trio be enough?" It depends on three things — how many people are coming, how big the room is, and whether there will be dancing. The tables below are based on what actually works.

The key figure: our own active Bose PA reliably covers an event of up to 80 to 100 guests. Above that we require a sound engineer and PA from the organiser. I say this openly, because an under-amplified band in a large hall sounds worse than a recording — and that serves nobody.

Floor area required

Line-upMinimum areaWhat determines it

These are minimums, not comfort. A trio will manage on a 3 × 2 metre stage, but the bassist cannot step back and the sound suffers. If half a metre is available, add it.

Rule of thumb

Band space vs guest space

Assume the band takes roughly the same floor area as two tables of eight. When you are planning seating close to the room's capacity, this is usually the thing that gets forgotten.

Hall acoustics vs restaurant acoustics

The same band sounds different in every space, and the difference is larger than most people expect.

High-ceilinged halls

Historic halls, galleries, hotel ballrooms. Long reverb, sound returning with a delay. The answer: a smaller line-up and less volume. A quintet in a baroque hall often sounds worse than a trio, because the individual instruments blur together.

Low-ceilinged restaurants

Short reverb; sound is absorbed quickly by soft furnishings and people. Less volume is needed, but the music has to be closer to the guests. A band tucked behind a pillar will not reach the far half of the room.

Outdoors

There is nothing for the sound to reflect off, so it disappears. Expect a shorter reach and music that covers only the area around it. For outdoor events we also require solid cover for the band and equipment — neither instruments nor PA may be exposed to rain.

When band size decides the programme

  • Will there be dancing? Without drums the floor will not fill. Quartet is the minimum.
  • Is it purely background? Duo or trio. A larger line-up adds volume, not quality.
  • Is the event formal and representative? A quintet with a horn section carries visual and sonic weight a trio cannot replace.
  • Is the space acoustically hard? Choose one size down from what the guest count suggests.

What to check before booking

  • Dimensions of the band area, and whether it is level and dry.
  • A 230 V / 16 A socket within reach (draw up to 1.5 kW).
  • Any noise limits and the hour by which the music must stop.
  • Whether the venue has its own PA and engineer, if you expect over 100 guests.
  • Loading access — a double bass and PA will not be carried up a spiral staircase.

Frequently asked questions

How many guests does your own PA system cover?

Our own active Bose PA reliably covers events of up to 80 to 100 guests. For rooms over 100 people and large outdoor productions we require a sound engineer and PA from the organiser.

Which line-up should I choose for 150 guests?

For 100 to 200 guests we recommend a quartet or quintet with PA provided by the organiser. Band size alone does not solve the problem without matching amplification.

How much space does the band need?

A duo needs at least 3 × 2.5 metres, a trio 3.5 × 3 metres, a quartet and quintet 4 × 3 metres. The surface must be level, clean and dry.

How does room acoustics affect the choice of band?

In halls with high ceilings and long reverb a smaller line-up sounds cleaner than a larger one. In acoustically hard spaces we therefore recommend going one size down from what the guest count suggests.

Do you play outdoors?

Yes, but outdoor events require solid cover for the band and equipment. Outdoors there is nothing for the sound to reflect off, so the reach is shorter than indoors.
