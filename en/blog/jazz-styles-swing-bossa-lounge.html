Jazz styles: swing, bossa nova, lounge

“A jazz band” can mean four very different evenings. The gap between swing and bossa nova is the gap between a full dance floor and a quiet dinner.

DK
David Kleňha
Jazz singer & bandleader · 7 min read · 8 August 2026

Style decides what the event does to guests — more than the individual songs do. What follows is practical: what each style does to a room, and when to choose it.

Swing

Classic jazz of the 1930s to 1950s. It has pronounced rhythmic drive — swing is the only one of these styles that reliably gets people onto the floor. It does need drums, so a quartet at minimum.

From the repertoire: Cheek to Cheek, It Don't Mean a Thing, L-O-V-E and Lullaby of Birdland.

Bossa nova

Brazilian jazz with a calm, even rhythm. The most reliable choice for receptions — no dynamic jumps, it does not pull attention, and guests converse easily over it. It works excellently as a duo or trio.

The classic example: The Girl from Ipanema.

From experience

One style all evening does not work

Three hours of bossa nova sends guests to sleep; three hours of swing exhausts them. A good programme has an arc: a calm opening, a middle section for food and conversation, an energetic close. That is dramaturgy, not repertoire.

Lounge and ballads

Slow jazz standards in chamber settings. Atmospheric music that fills a space without dominating it. It suits a hotel lobby, a reception, or the first block of an evening while the room is still filling.

For example Fly Me to the Moon, Summertime or My Funny Valentine at a slow tempo.

Latin

Samba, mambo, cha-cha and other Latin American rhythms in jazz settings. Livelier than bossa nova but less demanding than fast swing, which makes it an excellent bridge between background music and dancing. At an open-air summer event, latin is often the best choice of all.

From the repertoire: Sway.

Chanson

The French and Central European song tradition in jazz arrangements. Chamber-like, narrative, focused on lyrics. It works best in gallery settings and at events where people actually listen.

Frequently asked questions

Which jazz style is best for a reception?

Bossa nova. Its even rhythm has no dynamic jumps, so it does not pull attention and guests converse easily over it.

Which style gets guests onto the dance floor?

Swing. It has pronounced rhythmic drive, but it needs drums, so the minimum line-up is a quartet.

Can one style be played all evening?

We do not recommend it. A good programme has an arc — a calm opening, a middle section for food and conversation, and an energetic close.

Which style suits an outdoor summer event?

Latin. It is livelier than bossa nova but less demanding than fast swing, so it bridges background music and dancing well.

How many songs are in your repertoire?

Over 100, across swing, bossa nova, lounge, latin and chanson. We play without backing tracks and will learn a specific requested song to order.
