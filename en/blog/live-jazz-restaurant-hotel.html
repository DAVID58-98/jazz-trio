Restaurants & hotels

Live jazz for restaurants and hotels

Music in a restaurant has one job: lift the evening without shouting over it. A jazz duo or trio is the right fit for a hospitality space — acoustically and physically.

DK
David Kleňha
Jazz singer & bandleader · 8 min read · 8 August 2026

Restaurants and hotels are a different discipline from a corporate party. Guests did not come for a concert — they came for dinner. The music has to create an atmosphere people can sit and talk in, without disappearing entirely. That balance lives in decibels and in repertoire.

We play regularly at Hotel Augustine, Le Palais Art Hotel and Fairmont Golden Prague, as well as at Jazz Club Reduta. Here is how such a collaboration works in practice.

Which line-up suits a hospitality space

In a restaurant, floor area and volume decide — not band size. That is why the answer is almost always a duo or a trio.

Line-upFloor areaSuited to
Duo — vocals and piano3 × 2.5 mSmaller restaurants, hotel lobby, bar
Trio — + double bass3.5 × 3 mRestaurant with a function room, hotel evening, brunch
Quartet — + drums4 × 3 mOnly where dancing is expected

Drums do not belong in an ordinary restaurant — acoustically they overpower the room even when played softly. If the owner wants a full sound without volume, the answer is a double bass, not drums.

Set lengths and timing

Hospitality venues are not played as one continuous block. The format that works is two to three 45-minute sets with 15 to 20 minute breaks. The break is functional — it gives guests room to talk and staff room to serve.

  • Dinner service: start around 19:00, when the first wave of guests is seated.
  • Hotel lobby and bar: a later start, around 19:30 to 20:00.
  • Sunday brunch: two shorter blocks during the morning, quieter and purely for listening.

Volume, specifically

The rule I work to: at a table three metres from the band, it must be possible to hold a normal conversation without raising your voice. When guests start leaning towards each other, the music is too loud — whatever the meter says.

In practice that means heavily reduced monitoring, piano without harsh top end and vocals only lightly supported by the PA. We bring our own active Bose system, which covers a room of up to 80 to 100 guests. Larger rooms need a sound engineer on the venue's side.

From experience

The most common mistake venues make

The band gets put in the corner by the kitchen doors because that is where there is room. The sound then bounces off two walls and nothing reaches the far side. Even a smaller spot in the middle of the longer wall works better.

How a regular residency is arranged

A residency differs from a one-off event in three ways: frequency, repertoire and price.

  • Frequency. The most common patterns are one fixed evening a week, or one to two evenings a month. A fixed day has an advantage — guests remember it and start coming for it.
  • Repertoire. A residency cannot repeat itself. We have more than 100 songs, which lasts a season without a regular guest noticing a loop.
  • Price. Residency rates are set differently from one-off events — by frequency, set length and line-up. A single evening in a restaurant follows the standard price list, from CZK 10,000 for a duo.

What we need from the venue

  • Floor area according to line-up, on a level and dry surface.
  • One standard socket, 230 V / 16 A. Total power draw does not exceed 1.5 kW.
  • The ability to arrive 60 minutes before the start. Set-up and pack-down each take 30 minutes.
  • Somewhere outside the room to leave instrument cases.

Frequently asked questions

Do you play regularly at restaurants and hotels?

Yes. We play regularly at Hotel Augustine, Le Palais Art Hotel, Fairmont Golden Prague and Jazz Club Reduta. A residency is usually arranged as one fixed evening a week or one to two evenings a month.

How long does the band play in a restaurant?

Usually two to three 45-minute sets with breaks of 15 to 20 minutes. The breaks give room to both the service staff and the guests.

Isn't a live band too loud for a restaurant?

Not if it is played correctly. Our rule is that at a table three metres from the band a normal conversation must be possible. As a duo or trio we play without drums, so volume stays fully under control.

How much space does the band need in a restaurant?

A duo needs 3 × 2.5 metres, a trio 3.5 × 3 metres. One standard 230 V / 16 A socket is enough; total power draw does not exceed 1.5 kW.

How much does live music for a restaurant cost?

A single evening follows our standard price list — from CZK 10,000 for a duo and CZK 16,000 for a trio, for three 45-minute sets including the PA system. Residency rates are agreed individually according to frequency and set length.
